# Discovery Positioning: Agent-Mediated, Intent-Driven Social Discovery

Discovery tools sort along two axes: **what it matches on** (identity vs. intent) and **who controls the match** (centralized vs. decentralized).

## The Matrix

|  | **Centralized** | **Decentralized** |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity-driven** | **LinkedIn** — works for the platform, not users, and asks for payment in exchange for performance | **Bluesky · Nostr** — gave users ownership without changing how frustrating discovery feels |
| **Intent-driven** | **AI matchmakers (Boardy, etc)** — a concierge wrapper that still works for the platform, at a steep price | **Index Network** — your agent negotiates with the other humans' agents, nobody in the middle, nothing to broadcast |

## Index Network's Category

Index Network occupies the **agent-mediated, intent-driven, decentralized** quadrant:

- **Agent-mediated**: Two personal agents negotiate directly on behalf of their users; no platform intermediary decides the outcome
- **Intent-driven**: Matching happens on what people are actually *looking for*, not static identity profiles
- **Decentralized**: Control stays with the user — their agent acts as their delegate, not the platform's tool

This is fundamentally different from:
- **LinkedIn's model**: centralized identity + platform control = vendor lock-in + pricing extraction
- **Bluesky/Nostr model**: decentralized identity + same discovery UX = ownership without solving the problem
- **AI matchmakers model**: centralized intent + platform control = still extractive, just with better UX wrapped around it

Index flips the lever: your agent owns the match decision, acts only on your explicit intent, and never broadcasts to a central system. The other person's agent does the same. The outcome is symmetrical, auditable, and costs nothing.
