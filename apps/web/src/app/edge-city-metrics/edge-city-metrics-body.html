Residents kept using their agents

Residents sent 4,866 recorded messages to hosted agents over four weeks, and behind those messages agents ran 11,593 negotiation sessions on Index. The negotiation layer stayed fast and selective: 82% of sessions ended within two exchanges, and the median session lasted 4.9 seconds for accepted connections. The humans were the slow part, by design: median time from discovery to both people saying yes was 20 hours.

Opportunity pipeline

A resident files an intent. Agents negotiate in the background, continuously, to decide which introductions are worth a human's time — 9,688 opportunities detected in total. Both people accept before a conversation opens.

505 intents produced 9,688 opportunities, roughly 19 for every intent filed. Selectivity is the point: weak matches die in agent negotiation before they cost anyone's attention. No group of 239 people evaluates nine and a half thousand candidate introductions in a month by hand.

What people asked for

67% of sought connections crossed backgrounds or social clusters. About 4% of intents touched sensitive personal territory such as grief, identity, or spiritual seeking, served through an incognito mode. Around twenty residents wrote explicit policies for how their agents should negotiate on their behalf, unprompted.
