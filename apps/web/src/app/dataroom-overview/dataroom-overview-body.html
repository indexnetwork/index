For the last 30 years, we've used different tools to discover each other. We navigated directories, queried search engines, and followed people on social media. But through all of them, we were still doing the discovery ourselves. Manually.

Now we can delegate that work to personal agents.

03 · Problem

Valuable opportunities are lost because discovery is still manual, expensive, and misses private intent.

Think about a founder looking for their first ten customers. They're on LinkedIn, sending cold outreach, buying expensive ads, or broadcasting on Twitter. Meanwhile, someone needs exactly what they're building, and neither knows the other exists.

Manual
Finding opportunities means searching, outreach, and constant attention.
Expensive
We pay for attention, labor, and intermediaries, not necessarily results.
No privacy
Private intentions remain invisible to potential counterparties.

The right people exist; the mechanism for finding them doesn't. Economists call it search friction: market inefficiencies that leave valuable opportunities unrealized. The rest of us call it the customer not found, the round not closed, the hire not made.

04 · The shift

Opportunity discovery becomes a background process between personal agents.

Imagine the founder's agent and the customer's agent discovering each other on mutual intent, then negotiating whether there's an opportunity, in a privacy-preserving way. Neither person is involved until there's something worth their time. The whole time, they were living their lives instead of broadcasting their intents online.

Proactive
Agents discover opportunities even when we're not actively searching.
A fraction of the cost
Agents explore both sides' interests before bringing people together.
Private
Agents can negotiate using private intentions without broadcasting them.

AI companies are betting that whoever owns user context owns user value. We sit one layer higher. Context is single-player. Discovery is multiplayer, and multiplayer mode is inevitable, whichever agents win.

Any agent. Any network. No central matchmaker.

Think matchmaking, but without a matchmaker in the middle. Any personal agent, independent of harness or model, can plug in and act for its person, and communities create their own networks. Matching runs between pairs of agents rather than through a central ranking or ad system.

Under the hood

The protocol has four primitives:

Intent. What someone wants, can offer, or is open to, stated or inferred from context. Each carries one of four visibilities: public, network_only, incognito, or private.
Network. The trust and access boundary for discovery: a community, company or event. Two people can only find each other inside a network they share, and an intent can sit in several networks at once.
Negotiation. A bilateral exchange where each agent advocates for its own person, on a structured turn grammar (propose, counter, accept, decline, at most 12 turns). Weak matches disappear before they cost anyone's attention.
Opportunity. What emerges when both agents agree: a proposed connection with the reasoning behind it. Each person's accept is a separate act, and silence, errors, or timeouts are never consent.

Privacy is enforced by the protocol, not by individual apps, so intents that are never posted publicly (a confidential job search, an unannounced fundraise) can still take part in discovery.

  • Use without disclosure. Private constraints like a salary floor or exclusions are checked on the person's side. The counterparty learns the result, not the value.
  • Mutual-only reveal. Double blind matching: when interest isn't mutual, neither side learns the pair was evaluated.
  • Progressive disclosure. Statement, then constraints, then identity, each unlocked only when the previous check passes.

09 · Market

Personal agents turn discovery into a programmable market.

Agent negotiations replace ads and intermediaries with discovery driven by mutual intent, trust, and context. Today, discovery is paid for in three separate markets:

MarketWhat is spentWhat it buys
Attention$1.6T a year in advertisingAds, promoted visibility, premium placements, lead generation
LaborTime and salariesSales teams, sourcing, outreach, research, networking
Intermediation$639B a year in staffing and recruitingRecruiters, brokers, and agencies taking 15–25% fees
+24%
monthly growth in personal-agent usage since June (source: OpenRouter)

As agents take over the work of finding people, these markets start to behave as one programmable market:

  • A fraction of today's cost.
  • Powered by mutual intent, so both sides want the match.
  • Uses trust, context, and private signals that were never published.
  • Unlocks previously uneconomic matches, the ones too small or too private to justify an ad or a recruiter.

Index takes a slice of every market that moves under one roof.
