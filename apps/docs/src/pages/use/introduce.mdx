# Introduce [Introduce people you know to people on Index]

**Introduce** is the page in the Index app where you connect someone in your
networks with someone you know who isn't on Index yet. When a member posts what
they want and one of your contacts could help, the pair shows up there. You
write why they should meet and wake both agents. The two people meet only if
both say yes.

:::info
Introduce is in preview. It lives at
[index.network/introduce](https://index.network/introduce), under **introduce**
in the app's menu.
:::

## How reach works

Index reaches a person in one of two ways:

- **Through a network.** Members of a network find each other through
  [discovery](/discovery). Being in the same network is the consent.
- **Through a person.** An introduction reaches someone through a member who
  knows them. The member's relationship with both people is the context.

An introduction reaches exactly one hop. Your introduction reaches your
contact. It never reaches your contact's contacts, and discovery never finds
your contact. Two members who already share a network never need you: their
agents find each other.

## Set up

::::steps

##### Open Introduce

Sign in at [index.network](https://index.network) and open **introduce** in the
menu.

##### Connect Google contacts

Click **Connect Google**. Google asks you to let Index read your saved contacts
and the people you've emailed. Use the Google account with the same email as
your Index account.

##### Wait for pairs

Your contacts sync once when you connect. After that, opening Introduce syncs
them again in the background whenever the last sync is more than a day old.
Anyone you remove in Google is removed from Index at the next sync. Pairs come
in as people in your networks post what they want. You don't need to come back
and trigger anything.

::::

## Pairs

Each pair is two short lines with ⇄ between them:

| Side | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Left | A member of one of your networks, and what they need, for example "Leo needs a payments design partner". |
| Right | One of your contacts, and who they are for this pair, for example "Dana ran checkout at Stripe". Marked **not on Index**. |

Under the two lines is a draft intro. Both agents read it, so edit it until it
says what you'd say. **Skip** hides a pair. To stop pairs for one person
altogether, mark them **never suggest** under [Your contacts](#your-contacts).

A pair is an opportunity nobody has opened yet. It has the same parts as an
[opportunity](/opportunity), but it isn't one: only you can see it, and your
contact has no seat until you wake it.

Pairs are found in the background, never while you wait:

- When a member posts a public [intent](/intent) in a network you share, Index
  compares it against your contacts.
- When your contacts sync, your new contacts are compared against the intents
  you can see.
- A screen then keeps only pairs where your contact's role or company makes
  them plausibly useful for what the member wants. It also writes the two lines
  and the draft.

Only people are matched: a contact needs a name and a personal address, not a
team inbox like `info@`. Contacts who are already on Index never appear, because
discovery already reaches them.

## Wake both agents

When you click **Wake both agents**:

1. Index looks your contact up on the public web and checks it found the right
   person.
2. Your contact gets an unclaimed seat. It belongs to no network, so discovery
   never returns it, and only your introduction reaches it.
3. An [opportunity](/opportunity) opens between the member and your contact,
   with your intro on it. The member's agent takes the first turn.
4. Your contact's agent [negotiates](/negotiation) for them from public
   information only. It never waits on them, because they haven't signed up.

Both agents treat your intro as a claim to test, not an instruction to follow.

## Progress

Introduce has two columns. Pairs are on the left. Introductions you woke are on
the right, under **Running**, each with where it is:

**woken → talking → agreed → met**

It can also end early as **no fit** or **went quiet**. Finished ones move under
**Done**, with a count of how many met.

| You see | You never see |
| --- | --- |
| Which stop the introduction reached | What either agent said |
| When it started | Whose turn it is, or how many turns there were |
| How it ended | Which side said no, or whether an agent or a person said it |

This is the only feedback an introducer gets, so it stays coarse. It still
shows which of your introductions turn into meetings.

## Your contacts

The **contacts** tab lists everyone Index read from Google. Only you see it.

- Search by name, company or email, and filter to **not on Index**, **on Index**,
  or **never suggest**.
- **Never suggest** keeps a contact out of every future pair and takes their
  waiting pairs off the desk. **Allow** undoes it.
- **Delete all contacts** removes the list and every pair made from it.
  Introductions you already opened stay. Nothing syncs again until you read your
  contacts again.

## When your contact hears

Your contact hears nothing until the member says yes. Then they get one email
in your name: "*you* introduced you to *member*". It contains your intro and
what the member is working on. Signing in with that email claims the seat as
their own Index account. Replying "stop" asks Index to delete it.

If the introduction ends as no fit or went quiet, Index deletes what it found
about your contact on the public web: their profile line, their links, and the
intents written from them.

## Who sees what

| Who | Sees |
| --- | --- |
| You | Your contacts, your pairs, and the status of introductions you made. |
| The member | Your intro and the negotiation, as with any opportunity. Never your contact list. |
| Your contact | Nothing, until the member says yes. Then one email from you. |
| Other members | Nothing. Your contacts are never shown to anyone and never enter a network. |
