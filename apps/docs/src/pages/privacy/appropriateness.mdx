# Appropriateness [A skill your agent uses to make better privacy decisions in Index]

Appropriateness is a separate skill your agent can use. It is a check the agent
applies to the decision in front of it. It works as a question asked of that
decision, and a yes is what lets the agent act.

- **Assignment:** Is it appropriate to assign this intent to this network, to
  reach the people that context is for? A job search belongs in a professional
  community, not a neighborhood group. A yes automates the assignment. A no
  leaves the intent out.
- **Visibility:** Is it appropriate to open this intent this far, to be found
  by those people and not by everyone else? A quiet job search can stay
  `network_only` while an open role goes `public`. A yes sets the visibility. A
  no keeps it narrower.
- **Disclosure:** Is it appropriate to reveal this next, to test the match and
  nothing more? Your field and timing can come out early, and your current
  employer only once the match holds. A yes lets the negotiator reveal it. A no
  holds it back.

See [Privacy](/privacy) for the model these checks apply to.

## Background

Appropriateness comes from Helen Nissenbaum's theory of contextual integrity
(*Privacy as Contextual Integrity*, 2004; *Privacy in Context*, 2010). It
treats privacy as neither secrecy nor control. Privacy is appropriate flow.

Every social context, such as healthcare, employment, or friendship, carries
norms for how information moves within it. A flow is described by five
parameters:

- **Sender:** who passes the information on.
- **Recipient:** who receives it.
- **Subject:** who the information is about.
- **Information type:** what it is, such as salary, location, or health.
- **Transmission principle:** the condition the flow happens under, such as in
  confidence, with consent, or in exchange.

A flow that matches its context's norms preserves integrity. A flow that breaks
them is a violation, even when the information is true or already public. The
same salary is appropriate to share in a job interview and inappropriate on a
first date.

When audiences from different contexts are flattened into one, those norms stop
applying. Erving Goffman described how people keep audiences apart (*The
Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*, 1959). Alice Marwick and danah boyd
named the failure context collapse (2011).

The three checks above apply these parameters. Your agent is the sender, and
you are the subject. Assignment and visibility decide the recipients.
Disclosure decides the information type and the transmission principle: what
is revealed, and which passed check it is revealed under.
