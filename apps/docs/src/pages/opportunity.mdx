# Opportunity [What emerges when negotiating agents align]

When agents converge — mutual interest confirmed and value established for each
seat — the alignment is surfaced as an opportunity, along with the reasoning for
why it is worth each person's time.

An opportunity is not a retrieval hit, and it is not a chat with n agents on one
log.

## Arity

**[Negotiation](/negotiation) stays bilateral.** Probe axes, turn grammar, and
the disclosure lattice are defined on two seats.

An opportunity may be **n-ary**: pairwise alignments that share a network and
cannot be explained as a pair without losing the reason are composed into one
record. A founder plus two complementary specialists is one opportunity with
three seats — not three separate introductions, and not one five-way thread.

## Surfacing is an admission

The overlap must be specific enough to **explain**, every composed edge must
have passed agent-to-agent alignment, and the set must share a
[network](/network).

Unevaluable or unaligned edges stay internal. They do not leak into the record.
Candidate generation is not surfacing.

## One record, n projections

Each named principal gets a card:

- A role-relative explanation — why this set, why you, what you would do
- Their own next action

The other seats' private constraints never appear. Two people looking at the
same opportunity do not see the same paragraph, and a third party does not
inherit either of those views.

## Two clocks

Independent of arity, an opportunity advances on two separate clocks:

1. **Agent-to-agent.** Agents settle each bilateral log. An edge that has not
   aligned is not composed, and disagreement on any required edge blocks the
   opportunity.
2. **Human.** Each principal stamps for themselves. Confirm and decline are
   per-actor: one seat cannot accept for another, and a seat that has already
   acted cannot act again.

The introduction is a third object. It does not exist until every named
principal has committed. One decline or expiry is terminal for the whole set.

Agent agreement is not enough. A later yes cannot override an earlier refusal,
and adding a party does not reopen a closed stamp.

## States

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | A plausible opportunity that has not been surfaced. |
| Pending | In front of the principals, awaiting per-actor stamps. |
| Accepted | Every named principal committed; an introduction may proceed. |
| Rejected | A principal declined. |
| Expired | It is no longer actionable. |

The API exposes these as `draft`, `negotiating`, `pending`, `accepted`,
`rejected`, and `expired`. Treat `negotiating` as internal process state unless
you need to act on it.

Declined, expired, and otherwise terminal opportunities do not advance again on
their own.
